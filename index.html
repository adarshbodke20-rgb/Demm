Structure & Themes

What Lies Inside the Book

A step-by-step progression from cosmic chemistry to human psychology.

CHAPTER I Cosmology

Earth Before Life

Examining the hostile, lifeless early Earth—volcanoes, oceans, and natural chemical shifts—showing how habitable conditions emerged through physical laws rather than pre-planned design.

CHAPTER II Biology

First Life & Evolution

Tracing primitive cells to complex minds—and challenging the myth of perfection. The world was not built to fit us; through millions of years of trial and error, we evolved to fit an imperfect world.

CHAPTER III Cognition & Society

Religion & The Birth of God

Exploring the MCQ-style brain that hates blank answers, the Parent-Child Analogy behind a cosmic protector, natural morality without fear, and how religious hierarchies shaped gender roles in history.

How My Thinking Began

Author's Note

My questions about God began when I was around twelve or thirteen. I grew up surrounded by religious beliefs, but I never accepted everything simply because people around me believed it.

Over the years, I explored different religious ideas, questioned them, and gradually developed my own understanding. Today, I identify as an atheist.

I have no personal conflict with God, religion or religious people. This book is not written to hurt anyone's feelings or challenge anyone's right to believe.

I am writing because these are my thoughts, questions and observations, and I want to preserve them somewhere.

I don't want my identity to be mistaken for the beliefs of the people among whom I live. Being surrounded by a religion does not make me its follower, and assuming otherwise would be a misunderstanding of who I am.

This book is my way of putting my thoughts into words, preserving them, and leaving behind a record of how I see the subject.

— Adarsh Bodke

About the Author

Curiosity Beyond Familiar Answers

Adarsh Bodke is a biology student from Maharashtra, India. His interests extend beyond his academic studies into science, human nature, philosophy and the many questions surrounding life and human existence.

He has a habit of asking one question about almost everything: Why?

"Why do we believe? Why do we follow? Why do we accept? Why do we consider certain things right or wrong? And, perhaps most importantly, why don't we question them more often?"

For him, asking why is not about disagreeing with everything, but about understanding things beyond their familiar explanations.

Anatomy of God is his first book, an independent writing project developed alongside his academic pursuits. It reflects his personal curiosity about humanity, belief and the origins of the idea of God.
